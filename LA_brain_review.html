Displays the proportion of studies reporting a significant association, both overall and stratified by covariate category. Hover or tap a cell to view the underlying studies; click a study name to locate it in the full matrix.

Sig. / tested: studies with ≥1 significant test of the pair in any adjusted model reported (either direction) / studies with an adjusted estimate. Where the significant test came from a model other than the most-adjusted one shown in the matrix, that model and estimate are listed under the study name. Unadjusted estimates and LA measures dropped by stepwise selection are not counted.

Covariate columns: the same fraction, restricted to studies whose model for that pair included the covariate group. Columns are evaluated independently, so a study counted under LV factors does not necessarily adjust for the other groups. Where a study's covariates differed between analyses, inclusion is judged for the pair's own model. Within a column, a study counts as significant if a significant test came from any model that included that group. Studies whose final covariates were not reported (Bianco 2022) are left out of the covariate columns. AFib refers to studies that either excluded participants with AFib or included AFib as a covariate. Silva-R/Kizer 2026 (this study) is not counted.

Exposure–outcome pair matrix

Click to collapseClick to expand

Each cell is one exposure–outcome pair, coded from the most-adjusted model the study reported. Hover or tap a cell for the estimates. Click any column name to sort; click again to reverse.

Associated: at least one test of that pair in the most-adjusted model was significant (p<0.05). Null: no test in the most-adjusted model was significant. Unadjusted: only unadjusted comparisons were reported. Not retained (stepwise): the LA measure was a candidate but was dropped by stepwise selection, so no estimate was reported. A “test” is each LA measure × outcome definition the paper reported in its most-adjusted model; the hover card lists every test, with significant ones in bold.

Infarct is MRI-detected: silent infarcts, lacunes, cortical infarcts, microinfarcts, and acute infarct volume in stroke patients (Homssi). Clinical stroke is adjudicated ischemic stroke (incident, plus TIA in Habibi; prevalent prior stroke in Bianco; recurrent stroke in Yaghi 2015; stroke at presentation and incident stroke in Trueick). WMH includes WMH volume or load, white matter grade, Fazekas and ARWMC scores, and abnormal white matter volume. Cognition is neuropsychological test performance; Δ marks studies that also tested change over follow-up. Dementia is adjudicated incident dementia. DWI covers diffusion measures (fractional anisotropy, free water).

Mean age is in years (± SD). Infarct prevalence is the share of the analysed sample with ≥1 MRI-detected infarct, shown only for studies with an infarct outcome; Tan shows lacunes, Johansen shows lacunar infarcts (cortical 10%; overall not reported), Homssi enrolled only acute stroke patients, and Barnes, Masini, Maheshwari and Mannina 2023 show incident clinical ischemic stroke over follow-up (Yaghi 2015 shows recurrent ischemic stroke among first-stroke patients; Trueick shows incident stroke in a suspected-stroke cohort). Hover a value for its definition.
